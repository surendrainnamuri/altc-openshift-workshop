REFLECTION & REVIEW - CAPSTONE PROJECT PRESENTATIONS

Purpose

This capstone brings the Day01-Day05 learning into one production-readiness proposal. Teams present a realistic application or service design, defend its operational choices, respond to an incident, and identify the next validation step. The goal is evidence-based reasoning, not a perfect architecture diagram.

Recommended duration: 90-120 minutes
Suggested format: teams of two to four; 10 minutes presentation, 5 minutes questions per team

======================================================================
PART 1 - CAPSTONE BRIEF
======================================================================

Choose one scenario or an approved real service with all sensitive information removed:

A. Online retailer checkout API with payment integration.
B. Regulated document upload and retrieval portal.
C. Event-driven inventory update service.
D. Internal analytics batch-processing service.

Your design must describe:

1. User journey, service objective, failure impact, and one measurable SLO.
2. Workloads, Services, Routes, ingress TLS decision, and inbound/outbound traffic paths.
3. State classification, storage choice, backup/restore method, RPO and RTO.
4. Deployment automation source of truth, image-digest evidence, promotion and rollback approach.
5. RBAC identities for humans, CI, and deployment automation.
6. NetworkPolicy intent and dependencies including DNS/essential egress.
7. Resource requests/limits, scaling assumptions, dashboard/alert signals, and a runbook entry.
8. Developer design considerations: probes, arbitrary UID, configuration, Secret handling, retries/idempotency, graceful shutdown.

======================================================================
PART 2 - REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
======================================================================

Prepare these concise artefacts:

- One architecture diagram showing client -> Route -> Service -> workload -> state/dependencies.
- One deployment or Template/GitOps excerpt, with secrets removed.
- One NetworkPolicy or traffic matrix.
- One metrics/logs/events incident evidence panel or mock evidence table.
- One RBAC access matrix for observer, CI, deployment identity, and on-call responder.
- One backup/restore or failure-recovery sequence.

Label every assumption. A diagram is useful only when components, directions, trust boundaries, and ownership are clear.

======================================================================
PART 3 - PRESENTATION FLOW
======================================================================

1. Explain the user journey and failure impact.
2. Show the state and traffic design.
3. Explain how an immutable artifact moves to production.
4. Explain access and network boundaries.
5. Demonstrate how the team would detect and investigate one failure.
6. State one trade-off, one unknown, and the next validation experiment.

======================================================================
PART 4 - INCIDENT CARD
======================================================================

At the end of the presentation, the facilitator selects one card. Respond using: symptom, evidence, hypothesis, one safe action, recovery proof, and prevention.

Card A: Pods are Ready but the public Route returns 503 after a NetworkPolicy rollout.
Card B: A worker processes the same message twice after a timeout and restart.
Card C: A release uses a new image that is unavailable after an old PVC is mounted.
Card D: CPU usage is high, latency is rising, and the HPA has stopped scaling due to quota.
Card E: A contractor asks for cluster-admin because they cannot see production logs.
Card F: A database backup exists but the team has never restored it.

======================================================================
PART 5 - PEER REVIEW RUBRIC
======================================================================

Score each area 1 (missing), 2 (partially defined), or 3 (clear, feasible, evidence-based):

- User impact and service objective
- State/storage/recovery design
- Route, TLS, Service and NetworkPolicy design
- Deployment automation and immutable provenance
- Resource, scale, metrics, logs and events plan
- RBAC and Secret handling
- Developer runtime/design readiness
- Incident response and recovery proof
- Assumptions, trade-offs and validation plan

Give one concrete strength and one actionable improvement. Do not recommend broader permissions, disabled security controls, or untested data changes as default fixes.

======================================================================
PART 6 - FACILITATOR QUESTIONS
======================================================================

- What fails when this pod is replaced? What remains durable?
- Which Route/TLS choice fits the data and certificate ownership model?
- Which exact NetworkPolicy dependency would break first if you introduced default deny?
- How do you prove the production image is the tested image?
- Which identity can deploy, and why is it separate from CI and humans?
- What does your readiness probe mean operationally?
- How do you restore data, and when was that last demonstrated?
- What metric tells you a user is affected before a node is full?
- Which design assumption has the highest production risk?

======================================================================
PART 7 - FINAL REFLECTION
======================================================================

Write three sentences:

1. The strongest readiness control in our design is __________________.
2. The production risk we must validate next is ______________________.
3. The operational practice we will adopt is ________________________.

Summary

Production readiness is the ability to operate safely through normal change and abnormal failure. A strong design makes state, traffic, identity, observability, recovery, and ownership explicit—and proves its important assumptions before customers are affected.
