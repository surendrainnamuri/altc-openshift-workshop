REFLECTION & REVIEW - OPERATIONS, ACCESS, AND DELIVERY DISCUSSION

Purpose

This review consolidates Day04 into one operational story. Work individually or in groups of two or three. The aim is to practise evidence-first decisions: observe, correlate, make one safe change, verify recovery, and improve the system.

Recommended duration: 60-90 minutes

PART 1 - TRACE THE INCIDENT
======================================================================

Scenario

At 12:05 UTC, customers report slow checkout. `orders-api` is Ready, but p95 latency rises from 180 ms to 2.8 s and some requests time out. A deployment occurred at 11:50. One team member suggests restarting all pods; another suggests doubling every limit.

Create an investigation timeline. For each item, state what it can prove and what it cannot prove:

1. Deployment revision and image digest
2. Pod readiness and restart count
3. CPU/memory metrics
4. Request rate, error rate, and latency graph
5. Pod events
6. Application logs

PART 2 - LIVE CONSOLE AND CLI WALKTHROUGH
======================================================================

In a sandbox project, choose a deployment and complete the following without changing it:

- Find its desired and available replicas in the Web Console.
- Record the active rollout revision and container image reference.
- Open one pod's Metrics and select a time range that contains a baseline.
- Open Logs and Events for that pod.
- Reproduce the main observations with:

oc describe deployment/<name>
oc get pods -l app=<label>
oc describe pod/<pod-name>
oc logs pod/<pod-name> -c <container> --since=30m
oc get events --sort-by=.lastTimestamp
oc adm top pod <pod-name> --containers

Discussion: Which result was more precise in CLI? Which relationship was easier to see in the Console? Did permissions or configuration hide any view?

PART 3 - FAILURE DOMAIN CARDS
======================================================================

For each card, name the likely first evidence source, the first safe command, and a correction that would be unsafe.

A. Pod remains Pending after a release.
B. Pod restarts with OOMKilled.
C. Route returns 503 while pods are Ready.
D. Image pull fails after a registry credential rotation.
E. A contractor cannot see project logs.
F. A template change works in test but not production.

Suggested themes

A: events, quota, PVCs, scheduling; do not delete unrelated workloads.
B: termination state, previous logs, memory profile; do not blindly increase all limits.
C: service endpoints, route, readiness, network, application dependency; do not assume Ready proves request success.
D: events and image pull identity; do not paste registry tokens into a ticket.
E: RBAC and data classification; do not grant cluster-admin.
F: rendered manifests and drift comparison; do not edit production repeatedly until it works.

======================================================================
PART 4 - DEPLOYMENT AUTOMATION REVIEW
======================================================================

Review a Template, Helm chart, Kustomize overlay, or GitOps definition used by your organisation. Answer:

- What is the declared source of truth?
- How are environment differences represented?
- Is the deployed image immutable and recorded by digest?
- Which resources have more than one possible reconciler?
- Are Secrets referenced safely rather than rendered into logs or source?
- What schema, policy, and smoke-test checks happen before promotion?
- How is emergency production drift captured back into reviewed desired state?

Write one improvement with an owner and due date.

======================================================================
PART 5 - ACCESS REVIEW
======================================================================

For each subject below, propose the minimum project access, scope, duration, and validation approach:

1. A release observer who needs deployment and log visibility for one week.
2. A CI pipeline that pushes an image but never deploys it.
3. A deployment automation identity for one production namespace.
4. An on-call responder who needs emergency diagnostic access.

For every proposal explain:

- Why a human or ServiceAccount identity is appropriate.
- Why the role is namespace- or cluster-scoped.
- Whether Secret, exec, port-forward, or mutation permissions are needed.
- Who approves, audits, and removes the access.

======================================================================
PART 6 - LOCAL TOOLING DESIGN
======================================================================

Your service works locally but fails in OpenShift because it writes to a root-owned directory. Design the fastest safe feedback loop.

Include:

- local non-root image test;
- a sandbox project deployment of the same immutable image;
- the pod event/log evidence you would collect;
- the image change you would make;
- why changing the workload to root is a poor default;
- how developers can use temporary port-forward access without exposing the service broadly.

======================================================================
PART 7 - OPERATIONAL BEST PRACTICES DISCUSSION
======================================================================

Agree a team position on these practices:

- Every alert has an owner, severity, runbook, and recovery signal.
- A dashboard is evidence only when its query, labels, unit, aggregation, and time range are understood.
- Logs and events are collected with data minimisation and retention controls.
- Production changes use reviewed declarative definitions and immutable images.
- Access is least-privilege, attributable, time-bound, and reviewed.
- Local tools accelerate feedback but do not bypass cluster controls or production data safeguards.
- After every incident, the team captures the corrective change and one prevention action.

Final reflection

Write three sentences:

1. The operational signal I will check before changing a workload is __________.
2. The access-control shortcut I will avoid is ____________________________.
3. The automation improvement I can apply to a real service is ____________.

Summary

Operational maturity comes from repeatable observation and controlled change. The strongest teams connect metrics, logs, events, configuration, access, and delivery evidence into one clear decision trail.
